#### Learning objective
# Importance of Fluid and Electrolyte Homeostasis
### Explain the physiological importance of fluid and electrolyte balance for cardiovascular, renal, and respiratory function.

--
### Importance of Fluid and Electrolyte Homeostasis
####

The cardiovascular and respiratory system is a system to maintain a liveable environment for body cells.

Body cells need...
- delivery of oxygen and nutrients.
- removal of waste products.

This is most efficiently done in...
- an environment that is not too acidic or alkalotic.
- an environment that is osmotically balanced (to prevent cells from removing or absorbing too much water, causing cell lysis)
- there is effective blood volume to transport material to all organs.

--
### Importance of Fluid and Electrolyte Homeostasis
####

To maintain fluid volume and electrolyte balance, a number of systems are involved.

- Volume receptors in atria of the heart.
- Carotid and aortic baroreceptors.
- Innate regulation in the kidneys.
- Cardiovascular and respiratory responses (cardiac output, vasoconstriction, minute ventilation).
- Behavioural changes.

<p>This water would also need to be taken up by the ECF, causing oedema.</p>

#### Learning objective
# Behavioural Mechanisms of Salt and Water Regulation
### Describe behavioural mechanisms involved in salt and water balance, including thirst and salt appetite as part of homeostatic regulation.

--
### Behavioural Salt and Water Regulation
#### Thirst
- Drinking water (or eating water containing foods) is the pathway to restore body water.
- High plasma and interstitial fluid osmolarity increases firing of osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus.
- This triggers the sensation of thirst.
<div style="margin-bottom: 20px;"></div>
<div class="fragment">
<ul>
    <li>Drinking relieves the sensation of thirst <em>before</em> blood osmolarity changes.</li>
    <li>It is theorised that receptors in the mouth and pharynx detect the cool sensation of liquid, and silence the thirst response.</li>
    <li>This is why patients going into surgery (who cannot drink large volumes) are given ice chips to suck on. It alleviates thirst without adding significant body water content.</li>
</ul>
</div>

--
### Behavioural Salt and Water Regulation
#### Salt appetite

- The hypothalamus can trigger a sensation of appetite for salt with low blood osmolarity.
- Linked to aldosterone and angiotensin.
- Not to be confused with the reward system, which can also drive an appetite for salty foods.

--
### Behavioural Salt and Water Regulation
#### Avoidance preventing dehydration
- Dehydration can cause a desire to seek shady, cool places.
- Observed in animals who seek shade during the peak of the day.
- Shaped societal habits, such as the siesta in near equatorial regions of the world.

--

# Potassium Homeostasis
### Discuss potassium homeostasis, its role in neuromuscular and cardiac function, and key regulatory mechanisms.

--
### Potassium Homeostasis
####
Potassium...
- is critical to transmission of electrical signals in nerve cells.
- is critical to muscle function.
- contributes to osmotic concentration, and therefore fluid balance.
- has a role in acid-base balance.

In the kidney, potassium is...
- filtered,
- reabsorbed, and
- secreted

--
### Potassium Homeostasis
####
At normal levels, more K<sup>+</sup> is filtered and secreted than reabsorbed.

Clearance of K<sup>+</sup> is around 10 to 20%.
<div style="display: flex; width: 100%">
    <div style="flex: 0 0 50%; padding: 0px;">
<p><b>Low K<sup>+</sup> levels...</b></p>
<ul>
    <li>Low plasma K<sup>+</sup> concentration.</li>
    <li>K<sup>+</sup> reabsorption increases.</li>
    <li>K<sup>+</sup> excretion decreases.</li>
</ul>
    </div>
    <div class="fragment" style="flex: 0 0 50%; padding: 0px;">
<p><b>High K<sup>+</sup> levels (hyperkalemia)...</b></p>
<ul>
    <li>Hyperkalemia stimulates the adrenal cortex.</li>
    <li>Causes release of aldosterone.</li>
    <li>Aldosterone acts on the distal nephron, increasing the Na<sup>+</sup>-K<sup>+</sup>-ATPase pump.</li>
    <li>This secretion of K<sup>+</sup> increases K<sup>+</sup> excretion.</li>
</ul>
    </div>
</div>

### Acid-Base Balance
#### Hypoventilation and hyperventilation

If the cause of hypoventilation of hyperventilation is something other than a chemoreceptor feedback, then this will shift pH away from normal.

<p><b>Hypoventilation</b> (Lowers pH)</p>
<div>
\begin{equation}
\textcolor{red}{\uparrow\mathrm{CO}_2} + \textcolor{blue}{\downarrow}\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightleftharpoons \textcolor{red}{\uparrow}\mathrm{H}^+ + \textcolor{red}{\uparrow}\mathrm{HCO}^{3-}
\end{equation}
</div>
<p></p>

<p><b>Hyperventilation</b> (Increases pH)</p>
\begin{equation}
\textcolor{blue}{\downarrow\mathrm{CO}_2} + \textcolor{red}{\uparrow}\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightleftharpoons \textcolor{blue}{\downarrow}\mathrm{H}^+ + \textcolor{blue}{\downarrow}\mathrm{HCO}^{3-}
\end{equation}

### Acid-Base Balance
#### Metabolic compensation (kidneys)

Kidneys can slowly adjust blood pH through changing excretion or reaborption of:

1. H<sup>+</sup> (directly alters blood pH).
2. HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> (bicarbonate buffer reaction).

### Acid-Base Balance
#### Metabolic compensation (kidneys).<br>Proximal tubule secretes H<sup>+</sup>, reabsorbs HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>
Kidneys filter equivalent of around ½kg of baking soda (NaHCO<sub>3</sub>) every day.

Most of this is reabsorbed in the plasma to maintain its ability to buffer CO<sub>2</sub>.

There are two mechanisms that allow for this in the proximal tubule.
